# Attendance pipeline review — direction fix, bugs, optimisation

Date: 2026-09-06/07. Scope: the whole project, with the in/out labelling
defect as the centrepiece. Evidence: the production export
`data/gpu6_export_20260904` (2026-09-02..04, 579 recognised passes, 107
person-days), the body-crop corpus under `data/persons`, and 1 531 recorded
corridor clips replayed through the real pipeline.

## 1. Summary

People were being labelled the wrong way, and recognised people were getting
no in/out at all, for three separate reasons, none of them in the face
recognition:

1. **Direction was decided from a 4.5-second rolling window** of the
   trajectory, latched for 5 s and then declared "stale". Anybody who crossed
   the line and paused — at the door, at the desk, talking — lost their
   verdict. 27 of the 41 recognised passes that produced no attendance in
   three days were exactly this (`stale(6s, was ENTER)` and the like). That
   is the "short travel / never recorded" complaint.
2. **U-turns were split across the two cameras.** Somebody who walks to the
   door area and comes straight back is seen leaving by the Exit camera and
   returning by the Entrance camera, each half confidently "line+depth
   agree". The arbiter chose the direction by which camera had the stronger
   *face*, a coin toss, and when EXIT won a person who never left was
   checked out. 14 such contradicted pairs in three days, and 55 more
   opposite-direction pairs 15–120 s apart that no window could join. That
   is the "in labelled as out" complaint; one inspected day lost 49 minutes
   of worked time, another 41.
3. **Depth-only verdicts trusted perspective.** A head walking sideways along
   the far end grows 3x without coming a step nearer the line and was called
   ENTER; synthesised head boxes (used when the detector misses the head) were
   a different size from detected ones and read as a change of distance.

The direction verdict is now a property of the **whole pass** (where did the
person end up relative to where they started), the arbiter resolves direction
on **direction evidence** and lets the **later-ending view** win a
contradiction while holding a group as long as the person is still in view,
and the state machine keeps every sighting it used to drop.

Beyond the direction work, three read-only reviews of the rest of the code
(web/API, core vision modules, services and scripts) produced 44 verified
findings, all fixed: among them enrolment open to any role, a stored XSS on
the admin console, blocking work on the event loop, an RTSP timeout that was
silently ignored so a stalled camera wedged its reader for up to fifteen
minutes, the enrolment holding the SQLite write lock through a GPU pass, and
a debug-capture cap that reset on every restart.

On 23 hand-labelled corridor tracks the old rule was right on 13, refused 9
and was wrong on 1; the new rule is right on all 23. Replaying the export's
579 passes through the new decision logic turns the three inspected wrong
check-outs into re-sightings and recovers the lost worked time. The test
suite went from 410 to 489 tests, with new suites that pin every case
found.

## 3. Root causes

| # | cause | where |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Trajectory kept only the last 90 points; the verdict was "the last sign change" inside that window; a 5 s latch then expired it as stale | `app/core/direction.py`, `app/core/pipeline.py` |
| 2 | The arbiter's winner (most agreeing frames, then score) supplied both identity *and* direction | `app/services/arbiter.py` |
| 3 | The arbiter closed a group 15 s after its first pass even when the same person was still in view on the other camera | `app/services/arbiter.py`, `app/services/worker.py` |
| 4 | Depth-only verdicts needed only total travel, not travel along the corridor; synthesised heads were a fixed fraction of shoulder width | `app/core/direction.py`, `app/core/pipeline.py` |
| 5 | A debounced pass was dropped without a row, so the record could not show a second entry inside the cooldown; an EXIT while already out left an earlier wrong check-out time in place | `app/services/attendance.py` |

## 4. Changes

### Direction (`app/core/direction.py`, `app/core/pipeline.py`)

* The trajectory keeps every point of the track in a growing numpy array
  (bounded at 100 000 points by 2:1 decimation). A ten-minute track is
  decided in well under a millisecond, so the live overlay still asks every
  frame (every fifth beyond 100 points).
* Verdict = net crossing: start side vs end side, each from the first/last
  clean points beyond a dead band of 0.012 of the frame. A track that visited
  both sides and ended where it began reports the side it ended on as
  `crossed and returned (inside|outside)`.
* Depth compares the first and last detected, un-clipped heads; synthesised
  heads are sized from the last detected head; a depth-only verdict needs
  vertical travel in the direction the size change implies.
* No latch, no staleness; `direction_max_age_s` is gone. The pass is decided
  once, in `_prune`, from all of it.

### Cross-camera arbitration (`app/services/arbiter.py`, `app/services/worker.py`)

* Identity: unchanged (most agreeing frames, then score).
* Direction: tripwire verdicts (`line…`, `crossed…`) outrank depth-only,
  which outrank none; among equals the view that **ended last** wins. The
  overruled view is named on the event row:
  `line+depth agree (ENTER) [over Exit:EXIT, ended earlier]`.
* Each worker reports the people its live tracks provisionally name; a group
  is not closed while its person is still live on either camera (capped at
  four windows). The arbiter counts `contradictions` in its stats.

### Attendance (`app/services/attendance.py`)

* A debounced pass is written as a `DEBOUNCED` row that moves no state;
  `rebuild` skips it like `DUPLICATE_VIEW`; the dashboard shows it as SEEN.
* An EXIT for somebody already recorded as out moves the check-out time
  forward (they were demonstrably inside just before), without crediting the
  unobserved interval.

### Core vision modules (from the read-only review)

* `app/core/stream.py`: the RTSP `stimeout` option was silently ignored by
  the bundled FFmpeg 5, leaving 30 s open/read timeouts and a 30-failure
  reconnect rule — a camera that stalled without closing the socket blocked
  the reader for up to a quarter of an hour. Timeouts are now capture
  properties OpenCV enforces itself, and the reader reconnects after
  `stale_after_s` of silence. The replay source's first frame no longer waits
  up to the length of the whole schedule.
* `app/core/head_detector.py`: NMS is per class; a head-and-shoulders person
  box no longer suppresses its own head (or the reverse).
* `app/core/pipeline.py`: detection runs down to ByteTrack's low threshold so
  its second association can keep an occluded person on their track; heads
  are still gated at `head_conf`. The unused face detector is no longer built
  per camera (a torch CUDA context and weights on a shared card).
* `app/core/recognizer.py`: a fixed-batch model pads its last chunk instead
  of failing on it. `app/core/gallery.py`: the per-threshold penalty cache is
  swapped atomically.

### Services and scripts (from the read-only review)

* `debug_capture.py`: the per-person cap is seeded from disk (it reset on
  every restart and per camera; one person had 242 sidecars against a cap of
  40) and `capture()` honours the free-disk floor.
* `enrollment.py`: embeddings are computed before the write transaction
  (it held the SQLite write lock through the whole GPU pass while the capture
  threads blocked), and re-enrolment keeps the admin-reviewed corridor rows.
* `scripts/enroll_from_live.py --commit` writes corridor crops with the
  `live:` tag and their floor instead of bypassing the floor policy.
* `runtime.py`: the end-of-day sweep runs at startup with catch-up, so days
  the service was down no longer stay INSIDE forever.
* `corrections.py`: the filesystem glob runs before the transaction; an admin
  void also voids the same walk's `DUPLICATE_VIEW` twin; `on_gallery_change`
  lets the web layer reload the gallery after a poisoning crop is removed.
* `maintenance.py` keeps voided events and resolved sightings (the labels).
  `reid_worker.py` no longer flushes a loiterer who hit the crop cap as an
  unknown pass. `augment.corridor_probes` honours `resolved_employee_id` and
  `visitor`; `_gallery_rows` filters inactive employees like `load_gallery`.
* Smaller: `migrate.py` uses the business date; `recorder.py` survives a
  failed writer; debug sidecars record the threshold actually in force;
  `reset_attendance.py` waits 30 s for the lock; `set_direction.py --line`
  no longer resets `min_travel`.

### Web and API (from the read-only review)

* **Security.** Enrolment (`/employees/add`, `POST /api/employees`) and the
  ops endpoints (`/api/gallery/reload`, `/api/debug/captures`) were open to
  any signed-in role; they are admin-only now, and the navigation hides them
  from everyone else. `json_script` and `escapejs` in the Jinja compatibility
  layer emitted employee names unescaped into `<script>` blocks and inline
  `confirm()` handlers — a stored XSS on the admin console; both escape like
  Django does. State-changing requests are refused when their `Origin` or
  `Referer` host differs from the request host; the session cookie is scoped
  to the deployment prefix and marked `Secure` behind TLS; `/logout` accepts
  POST; `_safe_next` also rejects a backslash; five failed logins from one
  address in a minute lock it out for a minute.

  The cross-site check first shipped comparing the `Origin` host against the
  `Host` header, and that refused the operator's own forms on the proxied
  deployment - adding a viewer account returned "Cross-site request refused",
  because the proxy forwards its own `Host` upstream. It reads the browser's
  `Sec-Fetch-Site` header now, which no proxy can rewrite, and falls back to
  the host comparison plus a `trusted_hosts` setting only for browsers that
  do not send it.
* **Event loop.** Six `async` handlers (void, unvoid, resolve, gallery
  augment/remove) called blocking corrections and gallery code directly,
  freezing every viewer for up to the 30 s SQLite busy timeout; they run in
  the thread pool now.
* **Correctness.** Browser enrolment tried to open a WebSocket route that
  cannot resolve (`/ws/camera/registration/`) whenever cameras exist, so
  capture never worked on a real deployment; it uses the webcam path. The
  post-enrolment redirect, the live feed's snapshot URLs and the forbidden
  page's link lacked the deployment prefix. The live console read stats keys
  the worker never emits (algorithm fps, dropped frames, latency, last frame
  were permanently "not available"); they are derived from what the worker
  does report. A dead camera kept streaming its last frame at 10 fps as if
  live; viewers now get a frameless `stale` notice and the MJPEG route pauses.
  The unknown-review page never preselected a resolved employee; the users
  page printed login times in UTC; the dead `/cameras/rtsp` and
  `/cameras/add` routes and their orphaned template are gone.
* **Performance.** One JPEG encode per processed frame is shared by every
  WebSocket and MJPEG viewer instead of one render per viewer per tick. The
  attendance day view indexed the debug directory once per request instead of
  one glob per event (177 events walked ~4.8 million directory entries). The
  attendance list is capped at 31 days in HTML (CSV export unchanged).

trajectories:

| | ENTER | EXIT | UNKNOWN |
|---|---|---|---|
| pre-2026-09-06 rule | 147 | 142 | 17 |
| current rule | 148 | 154 | 4 |

Against the 23 decidable hand labels (`bench/direction_labels.json`):

| | correct | wrong | refused |
|---|---|---|---|
| pre-2026-09-06 rule | 13 | 1 | 9 |
| current rule | 23 | 0 | 0 |

The 9 refusals were all long tracks (15–60 s) where the person crossed and
then stood still; the wrong one was a walk that never reached the line. Two
tracks were labelled undecidable and both rules are now judged on them:
a sideways walk along the far end (old rule EXIT, now refused) and a ByteTrack
identity switch (skipped).

### Event-level (`bench/replay_events.py` on the export)

The 579 recorded passes, with the directions the old pipeline gave them,
re-decided by the new arbiter and state machine:

| metric | recorded | replayed |
|---|---|---|
| fused groups whose views contradicted | 14 | 9 |
| NO_DIRECTION passes | 37 | 36 |
| DUPLICATE_VIEW passes | 39 | 37 |
| check-out followed by the person walking in within 2 min | 32 | 34 |
| person-days NO_CHECKIN / NO_CHECKOUT | 5 / 20 | 4 / 20 |

What moved, and why the two that look worse are not:

* The three inspected U-turn check-outs (persons 30, 51, 21) become
  re-sightings with direction ENTER; the real departures of 30 and 51 become
  the check-outs; their days recover 49 and 41 minutes.
* The "check-out then walking in" count rises by two because persons 17 and
  51 now have their check-out at the real time and their genuine one-minute
  absences become visible — before, those check-outs were hidden behind an
  earlier wrong one.
* Person 23's day ends with a check-out before the check-in because the
  second entry was debounced and never written by the old system; the new
  state machine writes debounced rows, so live this case resolves.
* This replay cannot show the direction fix itself — it re-uses the old
  verdicts. The 27 stale refusals are addressed by the trajectory change
  measured above.

## 7. Optimisations

* Direction is O(1) numpy per frame on a growing array instead of a Python
  loop over a deque; a track can now be an hour long without cost.
* One fewer detector session and torch context per camera.
* Enrolment holds the write lock for the upsert only, not the GPU pass.
* Corrections do their filesystem walk outside the transaction.
* The stalled-camera path fails in seconds, not minutes.
* Web layer: see 4a (blocking handlers moved off the event loop, one JPEG
  encode per frame shared across viewers, one debug-directory index per day
  view, a bounded attendance list).

## 8. Deploying

* No schema change. Restart the service. The compiled `app/core/*.so`
  modules were rebuilt from the edited sources (`scripts/build_native.py`);
  a deployment from source must rebuild them or delete them, or the old code
  keeps running.
* New things to expect in the log: `DEBOUNCED` rows (shown as SEEN),
  direction reasons of the form `crossed and returned (inside)` and
  `… [over Exit:EXIT, ended earlier]`, and `contradictions` in the arbiter's
  stats.
* `direction_max_age_s` no longer exists; a `.env` that sets it is ignored.

## 9. What is still open

* **A U-turn whose return leg is never recognised** (face turned away, or
  the second track never named) still leaves the outbound EXIT standing. The
  live-track hold catches the common case; linking the unnamed return track
  to the named outbound one — by time and geometry, or by body ReID — would
  catch the rest.
* **The two tripwires are at different points in the lobby.** A person who
  stops between them is inside for one camera and outside for the other;
  only crossing-based verdicts are used precisely so that standing people do
  not generate contradictions, and the reception desk sits on the outside
  half by design.
* **ByteTrack identity switches** corrupt a whole-track verdict the same way
  they corrupt a vote; one was seen in 306 tracks.
* **The labelled set is 26 tracks.** `bench/dump_trajectories.py` and the
  strips make labelling cheap; the disagreements between the two rules are
  the tracks worth labelling first.
* The 1 080p clips halve every head; the tracking and direction results
  transfer (detection runs at 960 wide regardless) but recognition rates in
  those dumps do not represent the 4K cameras.
